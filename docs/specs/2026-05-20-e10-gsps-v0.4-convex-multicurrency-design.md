## 4. The decisive empirical object — FX-variance-share sensitivity surface

This section is the iteration's load-bearing anti-fishing safeguard. A reviewer must read it before §8.

### 4.1 Why a naive "β > 0" is trivially true and is NOT a finding

The analyst's local-currency cost stream is `cost = Q × c × FX`, where `c = $0.01` is the verified flat per-query price. **If Q were fixed**, `cost` is a deterministic scalar multiple of FX and the realized variance of the cost stream is mechanically proportional to the realized variance of FX — a regression of cost-variance on FX-variance returns β > 0 by pure algebra. That is not a finding. Reporting it as one would be a fishing artifact.

### 4.2 The exact variance decomposition (no leading-order caveat — CLOSED in v0.3)

In E10, **Q is stochastic** — a simulated workflow process (§6). Y, X, and the decomposition are defined in **log-return space**. Because `c` is constant, `Δlog cost = Δlog Q + Δlog FX` is an *exact identity*, and the variance of a sum is exactly the sum of variances plus twice the covariance:

> Var(Δlog cost) = Var(Δlog FX) + Var(Δlog Q) + 2·Cov(Δlog FX, Δlog Q).

This is **exact**, with no higher-order term and no approximation (Model QA Algebra note, confirmed in the closure re-review). The level-variance object `Var(cost)` has no clean additive split and is **banned** from the §4.4 share (§5.3).

### 4.3 The FX-variance share is a calibration-conditional sensitivity surface (CLOSED in v0.3)

The FX-variance share is

> FX-variance share = Var(Δlog FX) ÷ Var(Δlog cost).

`Var(Δlog FX)` is real (central-bank data). `Var(Δlog Q)` and the covariance are simulator outputs. The share therefore depends on the Q-volume calibration. The honest treatment: the FX-variance share is **NOT a single number**. It is a **calibration-conditional sensitivity SURFACE** — the share reported as a function across the defended (anchored) Q-volume range of §6.2. **The surface is the primary deliverable of E10 v0.4** (§9 SURFACE-PRODUCED). Statements about it are range-explicit: "FX volatility is the dominant cost-stream component *for Q-volume in [the anchored range]*."

### 4.4 The material threshold — the fitted hedge's break-even, pinned EX ANTE (resolves Model QA C-2 / N-2)

> **Reference:** Model QA C-2 PARTIALLY-CLOSED + N-2 (v0.3's §13.2 fits the convex payoff to the §4 *decomposition estimate*, making the §8 threshold depend on a Stage-1 result — a post-data-threshold path that breaches the Stage-1/Stage-2 firewall); CORRECTIONS-E10-4 Fix 2.
> **Why ex-ante derivation:** the firewall exists to stop Stage-2 quantities from carrying Stage-1 *findings* into the Stage-1 verdict. A break-even threshold whose payoff geometry is fitted to the realized §4 decomposition is exactly such a leak — the threshold then moves with the data it gates. The fix: derive the threshold from option-premium economics *before* the Stage-1 surface is computed.

The material threshold is the **fitted hedge's break-even share** — the FX-variance share at which the convex payoff's expected return covers its stream-funded premium. v0.4 pins it **ex ante**:

- The fitted-payoff **strike/range geometry and premium fraction** are pinned to an **ex-ante exposure assumption** declared in §13.2 — *not* to the realized §4 decomposition. The ex-ante exposure assumption is a stated, frozen prior on the representative analyst's FX-vol exposure structure, declared in §13 before E10.2 runs the decomposition.
- The break-even share is then derived from **option-premium economics** — the expected convex payoff as a function of realized FX variance and the (ex-ante-pinned) strike/range geometry, set against the (ex-ante-pinned) premium fraction. It is a Stage-2 economic quantity, computed and frozen **before the Stage-1 surface is computed**.
- The break-even uses an **ideal-scenario premium** (§13.4 — no market FX-stablecoin options venue exists in 2026); this is stated as a threshold caveat and is acceptable for the demonstration-grade/descriptive posture.

The threshold is therefore a pinned economic constant that carries **no information from the Stage-1 data** — the firewall is restored. The naive β > 0 sign check (§8 field 1) is retained only as a *necessary-not-sufficient descriptive* gate; the surface-vs-break-even comparison is the sufficient descriptive object.

## 5. Y — the exposure (realized variance of the cost stream)

### 5.1 Decision-citation block — Y choice

> **Reference:** CORRECTIONS-E10-2 §0.2 (cost-level → cost-variance reframe); §4 (the deliverable is the FX-variance-share surface); Model QA S-4 (Y must be pinned to log space for the decomposition to be exact); `dev_ai_cost_model_evaluation.md` §4.
> **Why realized cost-stream variance in log space:** the convex hedge protects the *variance* of the analyst's local-currency cost; Y must be a realized-variance object per (currency, month), in log-return space, matching X's construction so the decomposition is exact.
> **Relevance:** Y is computed from a simulated Q multiplied by a real FX path and the real $0.01 price — the FX path and the multiplier are real, Q alone is simulated.
> **Connection to the deliverable:** Y's total log-variance is decomposed per §4.2; the FX-variance share of that decomposition is swept into the §4.3 surface.

### 5.2 Y definition

For each (currency `cur`, month `t`): **Y = realized variance of Δlog of the representative analyst's local-currency monthly cost stream**, where the cost stream is `cost_τ = Q_τ × $0.01 × FX_cur,τ` over the sub-monthly index τ. Q is the simulated NHPP query-arrival process (§6); FX is the real central-bank rate path; $0.01 is the verified flat per-query price. Y's total log-variance is decomposed into the three §4.2 components.

### 5.3 Banned Y constructions

- A cost *level* as Y, or the level-variance `Var(cost)` — that is the v0.1/R5 directional object; `Var(cost)` also has no clean additive decomposition. Banned from the §4.4 share.
- A "counterfactual Mento-streamed equivalent" as Y — that is an M-side construction; using it as Y breaks the Stage-1/Stage-2 firewall.

### 5.4 Note on the fitted-hedge break-even (firewall restored)

The §4.4 material threshold is derived in the Stage-2 M-sketch (§13) from an **ex-ante exposure assumption** — not from the §4 decomposition. Stage-1 (§§3–9) and Stage-2 (§13) remain firewalled. The break-even number is derived in §13 from ex-ante-pinned payoff geometry and *imported into* the §8 pre-pin before lock as a pinned Stage-2 economic constant. It carries no Stage-1 estimation content. This is the v0.4 fix to the v0.3 firewall tension (CORRECTIONS-E10-4 Fix 2).

### 6.3 Realistic Q-volume patterns the intensity λ(t) must carry

The Q process is simulated via the R6 NHPP simulator. The NHPP intensity λ(t) is pre-committed to carry the following realistic modulations; the **magnitude** of each is what the §4.3 sensitivity surface sweeps:

1. **Diurnal + weekday/weekend cycle** — query arrivals concentrate in working hours and on weekdays.
2. **Burstiness / overdispersion** — dashboard batch-refreshes and research sprints cluster arrivals above pure-Poisson; the monthly-aggregate Q-distribution is overdispersed.
3. **Event-driven spikes** — market/protocol events trigger query bursts.
4. **Month-end reporting seasonality** — reporting cadence raises end-of-month volume.
5. **Slow secular workload drift** — the analyst's baseline workload trends over the panel window.

These five modulations are pre-committed before any simulation run. The functional forms (overdispersion mechanism, event-spike intensity law, drift parametrization) are locked in the E10.1 calibration note before any run (per Model QA N-5).

v0.4 pre-pins the Q–FX dependence structure: the simulator generates Q **independently of FX** in the primary specification (Cov ≈ 0 by construction), so the §4.2 covariance term is approximately zero in the primary. A behavioral-coupling arm (analysts cut queries in high-FX-vol months), with a stated sign and magnitude, is a pre-committed *sensitivity arm* only and does not move the primary.
