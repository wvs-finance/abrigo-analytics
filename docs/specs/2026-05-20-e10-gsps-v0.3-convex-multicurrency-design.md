## 3. X — the risk (realized FX variance, 100% real data)

### 3.1 Decision-citation block — X choice

> **Reference:** `memory/reference_bhaduri_laski_riese_concept_bridge.md` (Minsky uncertainty, not drift, is the PK micro-risk); CORRECTIONS-E10-2 §0.2 (directional → convex reframe); eight central banks' published daily FX series; RC BLOCK-1 (X is central-bank FX, independent of Mento token age).
> **Why realized FX variance:** the convex reframe makes the micro-risk the *unpredictability* of the FX rate. Realized variance of Δlog(FX) is the convex object a fitted convex payoff is priced against.
> **Relevance:** X is **100% real data** — central-bank rate histories, free, public, with verified 30-month free daily availability for all eight panel currencies. X carries no fantasy posture.
> **Connection to the empirical object:** X feeds the FX-variance term of the §4 cost-stream variance decomposition.

### 3.2 X definition

For each (currency `cur`, month `t`):

**X = realized variance of Δlog(FX_cur) over month t**, where FX_cur is the local-currency-per-USD rate from the relevant central bank's published daily series and Δlog is the daily log-difference. Realized variance is the within-month sum of squared daily log-returns (sum-vs-mean convention fixed at pre-pin). X is a non-negative convex object, constructed identically across all eight panel currencies so cross-currency variation identifies the relationship rather than confounding it.

### 3.3 The panel currencies

The panel is **8 currencies**: **COP, BRL, KES, NGN, GHS, ZAR** (EM, plausible Web3-analyst exposure, USD-priced data cost) plus **EUR, GBP** (developed-market controls). All eight have verified free 30-month daily central-bank FX history. The panel month axis is FX history, not Mento token age (RC BLOCK-1, C3-1). ## 4. The decisive empirical object — FX-variance-share sensitivity surface

This section is the iteration's load-bearing anti-fishing safeguard. A reviewer must read it before §8.

### 4.1 Why a naive "β > 0" is trivially true and is NOT a finding

The analyst's local-currency cost stream is `cost = Q × c × FX`, where `c = $0.01` is the verified flat per-query price. **If Q were fixed**, `cost` is a deterministic scalar multiple of FX and the realized variance of the cost stream is mechanically proportional to the realized variance of FX — a regression of cost-variance on FX-variance returns β > 0 by pure algebra. That is not a finding. Reporting it as one would be a fishing artifact: the relationship is true by construction.

### 4.2 The exact variance decomposition (no leading-order caveat)

In E10 v0.3, **Q is stochastic** — a simulated workflow process (§6). Y, X, and the decomposition are all defined in **log-return space**. Because `c` is constant, `Δlog cost = Δlog Q + Δlog FX` is an *exact identity*, and the variance of a sum is exactly the sum of variances plus twice the covariance, for any random variables:

> Var(Δlog cost) = Var(Δlog FX) + Var(Δlog Q) + 2·Cov(Δlog FX, Δlog Q).

This is **exact**, with no higher-order term and no approximation. v0.2 attached a "leading order" caveat to this identity; per Model QA S-4 and the Algebra note in `ModelQA_review_e10_v0.2.md`, that caveat was a **spec error** — it is **deleted** in v0.3. The level-variance object `Var(cost)` has no clean additive split and is **banned** from the §4.4 share (§5.3).

### 4.3 The FX-variance share is a calibration-conditional sensitivity surface (resolves Model QA C-1)

> **Reference:** Model QA C-1 (the share's denominator contains simulated Q-variance; the share is therefore monotone in a free simulator parameter — `∂share/∂Var(Δlog Q) < 0` — and routing it through the §9 ladder as an empirical number is circular).

The FX-variance share is

> FX-variance share = Var(Δlog FX) ÷ Var(Δlog cost).

`Var(Δlog FX)` is real (central-bank data). `Var(Δlog Q)` and the covariance are simulator outputs. The share therefore depends on the Q-volume calibration. v0.3's honest resolution: the FX-variance share is **NOT a single verdict number**. It is a **calibration-conditional sensitivity SURFACE** — the share reported as a function across the defended (anchored) Q-volume range of §6.2. The surface is the §8 primary object.

The honest resolution of the circularity: the share's denominator depends on simulated Q-volume, so the verdict is reported *conditional on the anchored Q-volume range* and never as an unconditional point. The verdict reads: **"FX volatility is the dominant cohort risk for Q-volume in [the anchored range]"** — range-explicit. Across that range the surface is swept; the verdict ladder (§9) is defined against the *surface*, not a point.

### 4.4 The material threshold — the fitted hedge's break-even (resolves Model QA C-2)

> **Reference:** Model QA C-2 (the v0.2 0.30 floor was an unanchored round number; the correct floor has a natural economic anchor — the share at which the fitted convex payoff's expected return covers its stream-funded premium).

v0.2's 0.30 material-share floor is **retired**. v0.3's material threshold is the **fitted hedge's break-even share** — the FX-variance share at which the fitted convex payoff (§5.4, §13) generates an expected return that covers its stream-funded premium. Below the break-even share, the hedge does not pay for itself and a convex FX hedge is not the right instrument for the representative analyst; at or above it, the hedge is economically warranted. The break-even share is *derived* from the hedge economics (the fitted payoff's expected return as a function of realized FX variance and the strike/range geometry, set against the pinned premium fraction), not asserted. It is computed in the Stage-2 M-sketch (§13) and the derivation is recorded before the §8 pre-pin locks. The naive β > 0 sign test (§8 field 1) is retained only as a *necessary-not-sufficient* gate; the break-even comparison against the surface is the sufficient object.

## 5. Y — the exposure (realized variance of the cost stream)

### 5.1 Decision-citation block — Y choice

> **Reference:** CORRECTIONS-E10-2 §0.2 (cost-level → cost-variance reframe); §4 (the decisive object is the FX-variance-share surface); Model QA S-4 (Y must be pinned to log space for the decomposition to be exact); `dev_ai_cost_model_evaluation.md` §4 (the implemented R5 estimator yields only n=1 variance and cannot express vol-on-vol).
> **Why realized cost-stream variance in log space:** the convex hedge protects the *variance* of the analyst's local-currency cost; Y must be a realized-variance object per (currency, month), in log-return space, matching X's construction so the regression is variance-on-variance and the §4.2 decomposition is exact.
> **Relevance:** Y is computed from a simulated Q multiplied by a real FX path and the real $0.01 price — the FX path and the multiplier are real, Q alone is simulated.
> **Connection to the decisive object:** Y's total log-variance is decomposed per §4.2; the FX-variance share of that decomposition is swept into the §4.3 surface.

### 5.2 Y definition

For each (currency `cur`, month `t`): **Y = realized variance of Δlog of the representative analyst's local-currency monthly cost stream**, where the cost stream is `cost_τ = Q_τ × $0.01 × FX_cur,τ` over the sub-monthly index τ. Q is the simulated NHPP query-arrival process (§6); FX is the real central-bank rate path; $0.01 is the verified flat per-query price. Y's total log-variance is decomposed into the three §4.2 components.

### 5.3 Banned Y constructions

- A cost *level* as Y, or the level-variance `Var(cost)` — that is the v0.1/R5 directional object; `Var(cost)` also has no clean additive decomposition (Model QA S-4). Banned from the §4.4 share.
- A "counterfactual Mento-streamed equivalent" as Y — that is an M-side construction; using it as Y breaks the Stage-1/Stage-2 firewall.

### 5.4 Note on the fitted-hedge break-even

The §4.4 material threshold depends on the *fitted* convex payoff, which is determined in the Stage-2 M-sketch (§13). Stage-1 (§§3–9) and Stage-2 (§13) remain firewalled; the break-even number is derived in §13 and *imported into* the §8 pre-pin before lock — it is a pinned Stage-2-derived input to the Stage-1 verdict ladder, not a Stage-1 estimation object.

### 6.3 Realistic Q-volume patterns the intensity λ(t) must carry

The Q process is simulated via the R6 NHPP simulator. Per §6.2 and Model QA C-3 / W-1, the NHPP intensity λ(t) is pre-committed to carry the following realistic modulations; the **magnitude** of each modulation is what the §4.3 sensitivity surface sweeps:

2. **Burstiness / overdispersion** — dashboard batch-refreshes and research sprints cluster arrivals above pure-Poisson; the monthly-aggregate Q-distribution is overdispersed, not equidispersed (Model QA W-1 — a pure NHPP is equidispersed and would understate `Var(Δlog Q)`).
3. **Event-driven spikes** — market/protocol events trigger query bursts.
4. **Month-end reporting seasonality** — reporting cadence raises end-of-month volume.
5. **Slow secular workload drift** — the analyst's baseline workload trends over the panel window.

These five modulations are pre-committed before any simulation run. Their magnitudes are calibrated to the §6.2 anchor and swept across the §6.2 bracketed range — the sweep IS the §4.3 surface.

### 13.2 The hedge is a fitted convex payoff, statically replicated (resolves Model QA C-2)

v0.2 fixed M to a single long-gamma straddle. v0.3 makes M a **target convex payoff fitted to the representative analyst's FX-vol exposure structure**, then **statically replicated by a portfolio of Panoptic options** — a collection of options spans any target payoff (static replication). The fitted payoff is determined by the exposure structure the §4 decomposition reveals; its expected return as a function of realized FX variance, set against the pinned stream-funded premium, defines the **break-even FX-variance share** (§4.4) — the share at which the fitted hedge pays for itself. The wallet maintains two flows: outflow-A to data services (the consumption stream), outflow-B to the fitted-payoff premium escrow (the hedge stream). The fitted convex payoff pays out when realized FX variance is high, exactly when the analyst's local-currency cost-stream variance is high.
