### 0.2 CORRECTIONS-E10-2 — directional → convex; Colombia-only → multi-currency

## 2. Cohort definition

### 2.1 Decision-citation block — cohort filter

> **Reference:** CLAUDE.md framework (investor classes exposed to a hedgeable micro-risk on a productive-investment path); `x402_subgraph_design_space.md` (Dune-Plus crossover at $390; free-tier qualitative limits); `integration_blockers.md` §1 (Mento 15-currency live panel + c→m rebrand).
> **Why this filter:** the operative cohort is the *middle band* of Web3 data analysts — those whose query volume is high enough that the free tier is qualitatively inadequate, yet low enough that they have not crossed into a flat Dune-Plus subscription. Only the middle band has a *volume-sensitive, USD-priced, FX-exposed* cost stream; below `Q_low` the analyst pays nothing, above `Q_high` the cost is a fixed subscription with no per-query FX exposure.
> **Relevance:** the band `[Q_low, Q_high]` defines who carries the hedgeable risk; both bounds are parameters of the §6 simulation, not empirical filters.
> **Connection to verdict:** the simulator (§6) emits one representative analyst per currency whose monthly query volume Q is drawn inside `[Q_low, Q_high]`; outside the band the iteration has no risk object and the verdict is undefined by construction.

### 2.2 The cohort and the query-volume band

Cohort: Web3 data analysts resident in any country with a live Mento-issued FX stablecoin, with monthly data-query volume Q in the middle band `Q_low ≤ Q ≤ Q_high`.

- **Q** = monthly data-query volume (queries/month). The simulated quantity.
- **Q_high = 39,000 queries/month.** Derived as $390 ÷ $0.01/query — the point at which pay-per-query cost crosses the Dune-Plus flat subscription, above which a rational analyst switches to the subscription and the per-query FX channel vanishes. **Q_high is pinned off the model cap of $390, not the observed $399 Dune price** (see §2.3 calibration note).
- **Q_low** = the **qualitative-limit threshold** — the query volume at which the free tier becomes suboptimal because of its non-numeric limits: rate caps, no `archive` / `trace_*` access, single-subgraph-only coverage, no cross-protocol curation. `Q_low` is NOT the numeric free quota (100k queries/mo); it is a *behavioral* parameter capturing where the free tier's qualitative deficiencies bind, pinned in the §6 simulation, not read off a price page.

Currency panel: the live Mento FX-stablecoin set — ~15 currencies as of 2026-05 (USD, EUR, BRL, KES, COP, PHP, XOF, NGN, GBP, JPY, CHF, ZAR, AUD, CAD, GHS per `integration_blockers.md` §1.1). The practical E10 panel is the EM subset with a plausible Web3-analyst cohort and USD-priced data cost: **COP, BRL, KES, NGN, PHP, GHS, ZAR, XOF**, plus **EUR / GBP** as developed-market controls. The live set and the c→m rebrand status (legacy `c`-tokens still trade on-chain; `m`-suffix rollout mid-flight) are verified at E10.0 (§10).

### 2.3 Calibration note — $390 vs $399 Dune price

The Dune-Plus price is currently quoted at **≈ $399/mo** (third-party 2026 sources; the dune.com pricing page did not render to the research probe). The model-cap used to pin `Q_high` is **$390**, the figure carried in the v0.1 design and the brainstorm. The $9 discrepancy is recorded here as a calibration note: `Q_high` is held at 39,000 (= $390 ÷ $0.01) for v0.2; a sensitivity arm at `Q_high = 39,900` (= $399 ÷ $0.01) is permitted as a robustness check only and does not move the primary. This discrepancy is flagged for reviewer attention (§11).

### 2.4 Out of scope

- Analysts below `Q_low` (free tier fully adequate — no hedgeable cost stream) and above `Q_high` (flat subscription — no per-query FX exposure).
- Non-Mento-currency analysts — each would require a separate FX-stablecoin integration and lose the single-substrate advantage.
- Counterfactual cohort scaling ("if N analysts adopted x402") — the simulation emits *one representative analyst per currency* calibrated to that currency's market; it does not fabricate a cohort population.

## 3. X — the risk (realized FX variance)

### 3.1 Decision-citation block — X choice

> **Reference:** `memory/reference_bhaduri_laski_riese_concept_bridge.md` (Minsky uncertainty, not drift, is the PK micro-risk); CORRECTIONS-E10-2 §0.2 (directional → convex reframe); central-bank FX rate histories (Banrep TRM + peers).
> **Why realized FX variance:** the convex reframe makes the micro-risk the *unpredictability* of the FX rate, not its direction. A signed return is a directional object; realized variance of Δlog(FX) is the convex object that a long-gamma straddle is priced against. This aligns X with the M-side payoff shape (§5).
> **Relevance:** X is **100% real data** — central-bank rate histories, free, public. Only Q is simulated. X carries no fantasy posture.
> **Connection to the empirical object:** X feeds the FX-variance component of the cost-stream variance decomposition (§4); the FX-variance *share* of total cost-stream variance is the decisive object.

### 3.2 X definition

For each (currency `cur`, month `t`):

**X_{cur,t} = realized variance of Δlog(FX_{cur})** over month `t`, where FX_{cur} is the local-currency-per-USD rate from the relevant central bank's published series, and Δlog is the daily log-difference. Realized variance is the within-month sum (or mean, fixed at pre-pin) of squared daily log-returns.

X is a non-negative convex object, not a signed return. It is constructed identically across all panel currencies so that cross-currency variation identifies the relationship rather than confounding it.

## 4. The decisive empirical object — FX-variance share (anti-fishing-critical)

This section is given its own number because it is the iteration's load-bearing anti-fishing safeguard. A reviewer must read it before §7.

### 4.1 Why a naive "β > 0" is trivially true and is NOT a finding

The analyst's local-currency cost stream is `cost_t = Q_t × c × FX_t`, where `c = $0.01` is the verified flat per-query price. **If Q were fixed**, then `cost = (Q·c)·FX` is a deterministic scalar multiple of FX, and the realized variance of the cost stream would be *mechanically proportional* to the realized variance of FX. A regression of cost-variance on FX-variance would return β > 0 by pure algebra. **That is not a finding.** Reporting it as one would be a fishing artifact — the relationship is true by construction, independent of any economic content.

### 4.2 Why Q being stochastic makes the question real

In E10 v0.2, **Q is stochastic** — the analyst's monthly query volume is a random workflow process simulated by the §6 engine. With Q random and FX random, the variance of the cost stream decomposes (to leading order, in log-returns) into three parts:

> Var(cost stream) = [FX-variance component] + [Q-variance component] + [covariance term].

The FX-variance component is what a convex FX hedge can protect against; the Q-variance component is *idiosyncratic query-volume noise* that no FX instrument touches.

### 4.3 The decisive object — FX-variance share

The decisive empirical object of E10 v0.2 is the **FX-variance SHARE of total cost-stream variance**:

> FX-variance share = [FX-variance component] ÷ [total cost-stream variance].

The iteration **PASSES only if that share is material** — i.e. FX volatility, not query-volume noise, is the dominant component of the cohort's cost-stream risk. The share threshold is pre-pinned (§7, field 3). If the **Q-variance component dominates**, the honest verdict is that FX volatility is *not* the cohort's main risk, and a convex FX hedge is not the right instrument — this is a real, acceptable outcome, recorded as a HALT (§9), not engineered away.

The naive β > 0 sign test (§7, field 1) is retained only as a *necessary-not-sufficient* gate: a wrong sign would falsify even the mechanical channel, but a right sign proves nothing on its own. The **share** is the sufficient object.

## 5. Y — the exposure (realized variance of the cost stream)

### 5.1 Decision-citation block — Y choice

> **Reference:** CORRECTIONS-E10-2 §0.2 (cost-level → cost-variance reframe); §4 (the decisive object is the FX-variance share of cost-stream variance); `dev_ai_cost_model_evaluation.md` §4 (the implemented R5 estimator yields only n=1 variance and cannot express vol-on-vol).
> **Why realized cost-stream variance:** the convex hedge protects the *variance* of the analyst's local-currency cost, not its level. Y must therefore be a realized-variance object measured per (currency, month), matching X's construction so the regression is variance-on-variance.
> **Relevance:** Y is computed from a simulated Q (the only generated quantity) multiplied by a real FX path and the real $0.01 price; the FX path and the multiplier are real, Q is simulated — the fantasy-threshold posture is confined to Q alone (§7, field 5).
> **Connection to the decisive object:** Y's total variance is decomposed per §4.2; the FX-variance share of that decomposition is the §7 primary object.

### 5.2 Y definition

For each (currency `cur`, month `t`):

**Y_{cur,t} = realized variance of the analyst's local-currency monthly cost stream**, where the cost stream is `cost_τ = Q_τ × $0.01 × FX_{cur,τ}` evaluated over the sub-monthly time index τ. Q is the simulated NHPP query-arrival process (§6); FX is the real central-bank rate path; $0.01 is the verified flat per-query price.

Y's total realized variance is decomposed into the three §4.2 components. The regression LHS and the share construction both derive from this decomposition.

### 5.3 Banned Y constructions

- A cost *level* as Y — that is the v0.1 / R5 directional object; using it collapses the convex reframe.
- A "counterfactual Mento-streamed equivalent" as Y — that is an M-side construction; using it as Y breaks the Stage-1 / Stage-2 firewall.

## 6. Simulation engine

### 6.1 Decision-citation block — simulation posture

> **Reference:** `memory/project_e10_x402_substrate_pending_maturity_2026_11.md` (x402 substrate too young — 8 days old as of v0.1; 2026-11 re-check); `dev_ai_cost_model_evaluation.md` §5 (R6 NHPP spec is the asset to resurrect; R5 estimator unusable for vol-on-vol; ~25–30% reuse / ~70% fresh).
> **Why simulation-driven:** the x402 substrate is structurally too young to observe a panel of monthly cost variances empirically. The only way to obtain a {Var_cost[t]} panel is to simulate many monthly query-cost trajectories under real FX paths and stochastic query workflows. This is precisely what the R6 design was built for and the dev_ai_cost iteration's own PAUSE-PENDING-MORE-DATA confirms the wall.
> **Relevance:** the simulation generates **only Q**; X and the FX multiplier are real. The fantasy posture is therefore minimal and explicitly bounded (§7, field 5).
> **Connection to the engine:** resurrect the dev_ai_cost R6 NHPP workflow-simulation design — specced (`2026-05-16-r6-...-design.md` v0.1.3 APPROVED) but never coded.

### 6.2 Reuse vs build (per `dev_ai_cost_model_evaluation.md` §5)

**Reuse (~25–30%, low/zero lift):**
- The repo's three-tier functional-python discipline, dual-SHA pinning, audit-block convention, Hypothesis line-conservation property — engineering scaffolding.
- The `stochastic_fx` GBM / Jump-Diffusion FX-path generator family — the FX-side engine; here used to validate the real FX paths' variance dynamics and as a robustness comparator.
- The R6 NHPP design blueprint — NHPP within-session arrivals, hour-block × day-type seasonality grid, session-composition bootstrap, Monte-Carlo cost-trajectory ensemble — adopted as the architecture for the query-workflow simulator.

**Build fresh (~70%, the load-bearing core):**
- The R6 simulation engine itself as code (the R6 spec is a design + 19-task plan with zero implementation; `dev_ai_cost` ships only the R5 path).
- The **vol-on-vol estimator** — realized-variance-per-month construction on both X and Y, the §4 three-way variance decomposition, the FX-variance-share computation, and the share-on-threshold verdict logic. The implemented R5 estimator yields only one realized-variance observation (n=1) and cannot express any of this.
- The multi-currency machinery — currency-keyed FX panels, per-currency calibration, the cross-currency pooling in the regression.

### 6.3 What the engine emits

The simulator emits, **per currency**, the stochastic Q-process of **one representative analyst** of that currency, calibrated to that currency's market (typical analyst workflow, query mix, and the `[Q_low, Q_high]` band of §2.2). For each simulated month and each currency, the engine produces a monthly cost-stream trajectory `cost_τ = Q_τ × $0.01 × FX_{cur,τ}` against the *real* FX path, from which the realized cost-stream variance Y and its §4 decomposition are computed. The dev_ai_cost cap-wait / session-cap logic is discarded — x402 has no session cap, pricing is pay-per-query.

E4 and E9-A hedge a streamed-*in* receipt; **E10 hedges a streamed-*out* cost VOLATILITY** — the most general form, where the hedged object is the *variance* of a continuous-consumption liability rather than the level of a receipt. ## 13. M-sketch (Stage-2 firewall; ideal-scenario only; descriptive)

### 13.1 Decision-citation block — M anchor

> **Reference:** CLAUDE.md §"Ideal-scenario modeling permitted (Panoptic-liquidity caveat)" and §"Transmission mechanism — premium-funded ratchet"; E4 / E9-A streamed-liability M-family; `integration_blockers.md` §3 (convex hedge strictly ideal-scenario — no on-chain FX-stablecoin options venue exists in 2026).
> **Why this M anchor:** the convex reframe changes the payoff shape from a directional put to a long-gamma straddle (long-volatility), the natural instrument against realized-variance risk. The streamed-liability premium-funding mechanism is unchanged — this is its third instance.
> **Relevance:** M is descriptive only at Stage-2; deployment is firewalled to Stage-3 and is currently infeasible.
> **Connection to the empirical object:** a long-gamma straddle on the FX-stablecoin pair pays out precisely when realized FX variance — the §3 X — is high, hedging exactly the cost-stream variance the §4 share isolates.

### 13.2 Ideal-scenario M position (descriptive)

The Stage-2 M-sketch is a **Panoptic long-gamma straddle** on the FX-stablecoin pair (e.g. COPm/USDC, BRLm/USDC), struck near spot, perpetual and auto-rolled. Premium funding: a **second Superfluid CFA stream from the same wallet** that runs the data-consumption stream — the streamed-liability primitive, here cost-side and convex. The wallet maintains two flows: outflow-A to data services (the consumption stream), outflow-B to the straddle premium escrow (the hedge stream). The straddle pays out when realized FX variance is high, exactly when the analyst's local-currency cost-stream variance is high.

### 13.3 Explicit ideal-scenario caveat

This M is **strictly ideal-scenario** and **cannot be deployed in 2026**, per `integration_blockers.md` §3 and CLAUDE.md's Panoptic-liquidity caveat:

- Panoptic V2 launches on Ethereum mainnet first; there is **no Celo / Base / Optimism deployment**.
- There is **no FX-stablecoin Uniswap pool with real depth** on any chain — Mento FX liquidity lives in Mento's own FPMM / vAMM on Celo, which Panoptic cannot read; Mento's vAMM is not a Uniswap v3 concentrated-liquidity pool, so Panoptic's core primitive cannot attach to it.
- Panoptic V2 ships a Gamma Scalping Vault, so long-gamma is *mechanically* expressible — but no FX-pair venue exists for it.

The empirical β-validation work (§§3–9) is **independent of deployment** — it asks whether the FX-volatility risk admits a material variance share, which is answered by the §6 simulation, not by live LP capital. The M-sketch proposes the ideal settlement architecture; deployment is Stage-3 and out of scope. Stage drift (M-design ballooning back into apparatus) is anti-fishing-banned.
