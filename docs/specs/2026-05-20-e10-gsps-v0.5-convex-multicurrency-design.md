## 4. The decisive empirical object — FX-variance-share sensitivity surface

This section is the iteration's load-bearing anti-fishing safeguard. A reviewer must read it before §8. **Unchanged from v0.4** — the panel re-spec does not touch the decomposition object.

### 4.1 Why a naive "β > 0" is trivially true and is NOT a finding

The analyst's local-currency cost stream is `cost = Q × c × FX`, where `c = $0.01` is the verified flat per-query price. **If Q were fixed**, `cost` is a deterministic scalar multiple of FX and the realized variance of the cost stream is mechanically proportional to the realized variance of FX — a regression of cost-variance on FX-variance returns β > 0 by pure algebra. That is not a finding. Reporting it as one would be a fishing artifact.

### 4.2 The exact variance decomposition (no leading-order caveat — CLOSED in v0.3)

In E10, **Q is stochastic** — a simulated workflow process (§6). Y, X, and the decomposition are defined in **log-return space**. Because `c` is constant, `Δlog cost = Δlog Q + Δlog FX` is an *exact identity*, and the variance of a sum is exactly the sum of variances plus twice the covariance:

> Var(Δlog cost) = Var(Δlog FX) + Var(Δlog Q) + 2·Cov(Δlog FX, Δlog Q).

This is **exact**, with no higher-order term and no approximation. The level-variance object `Var(cost)` has no clean additive split and is **banned** from the §4.4 share (§5.3).

### 4.3 The FX-variance share is a calibration-conditional sensitivity surface (CLOSED in v0.3)

The FX-variance share is

> FX-variance share = Var(Δlog FX) ÷ Var(Δlog cost).

`Var(Δlog FX)` is real (central-bank data). `Var(Δlog Q)` and the covariance are simulator outputs. The share therefore depends on the Q-volume calibration. The honest treatment: the FX-variance share is **NOT a single number**. It is a **calibration-conditional sensitivity SURFACE** — the share reported as a function across the defended (anchored) Q-volume range of §6.2. **The surface is the primary deliverable of E10 v0.5** (§9 SURFACE-PRODUCED). Statements about it are range-explicit: "FX volatility is the dominant cost-stream component *for Q-volume in [the anchored range]*."

### 4.4 The material threshold — the fitted hedge's break-even, pinned EX ANTE (CORRECTIONS-E10-4 Fix 2 — carried forward)

> **Reference:** CORRECTIONS-E10-4 Fix 2 (the break-even threshold is derived from option-premium economics computed ex ante, independently of the §4 decomposition — restoring the Stage-1/Stage-2 firewall).

The material threshold is the **fitted hedge's break-even share** — the FX-variance share at which the convex payoff's expected return covers its stream-funded premium. It is pinned **ex ante**:

- The fitted-payoff **strike/range geometry and premium fraction** are pinned to an **ex-ante exposure assumption** declared in §13.2 — *not* to the realized §4 decomposition. The ex-ante exposure assumption is a stated, frozen prior on the representative analyst's FX-vol exposure structure, declared in §13 before E10.2 runs the decomposition.
- The break-even share is then derived from **option-premium economics** — the expected convex payoff as a function of realized FX variance and the (ex-ante-pinned) strike/range geometry, set against the (ex-ante-pinned) premium fraction. It is a Stage-2 economic quantity, computed and frozen **before the Stage-1 surface is computed**.
- The break-even uses an **ideal-scenario premium** (§13.4 — no market FX-stablecoin options venue exists in 2026); this is stated as a threshold caveat and is acceptable for the demonstration-grade/descriptive posture.

The threshold is therefore a pinned economic constant that carries **no information from the Stage-1 data** — the firewall is restored. The naive β > 0 sign check (§7 field 1) is retained only as a *necessary-not-sufficient descriptive* gate; the surface-vs-break-even comparison is the sufficient descriptive object.

### 6.3 Realistic Q-volume patterns the intensity λ(t) must carry

The Q process is simulated via the R6 NHPP simulator. The NHPP intensity λ(t) is pre-committed to carry the following realistic modulations; the **magnitude** of each is what the §4.3 sensitivity surface sweeps:

1. **Diurnal + weekday/weekend cycle** — query arrivals concentrate in working hours and on weekdays.
2. **Burstiness / overdispersion** — dashboard batch-refreshes and research sprints cluster arrivals above pure-Poisson; the monthly-aggregate Q-distribution is overdispersed.
3. **Event-driven spikes** — market/protocol events trigger query bursts.
4. **Month-end reporting seasonality** — reporting cadence raises end-of-month volume.
5. **Slow secular workload drift** — the analyst's baseline workload trends over the panel window.

These five modulations are pre-committed before any simulation run. The functional forms (overdispersion mechanism, event-spike intensity law, drift parametrization) are locked in the E10.1 calibration note before any run.

### 6.4 The Q–FX dependence (pre-pinned)

v0.5 pre-pins the Q–FX dependence structure: the simulator generates Q **independently of FX** in the primary specification (Cov ≈ 0 by construction), so the §4.2 covariance term is approximately zero in the primary. A behavioral-coupling arm (analysts cut queries in high-FX-vol months), with a stated sign and magnitude, is a pre-committed *sensitivity arm* only and does not move the primary.
