## 1. Task 2.5.1 — The ex-ante exposure assumption (FROZEN) `[brainstorm-judgment]`

### 1.1 Deliberation

The ex-ante exposure assumption is a stated, frozen prior on the representative
analyst's FX-vol exposure structure. Spec §11 open item 4 flags it explicitly
for user confirmation; it is load-bearing because it sets the verdict threshold.

The prior must answer one question: **across the 5 panel currencies' published
long-run FX-vol regimes, what share of the representative analyst's
cost-stream variance does the analyst ex-ante expect to come from FX
volatility?** This is *not* a realized measurement (that is the Phase-3 surface);
it is the analyst's structural belief, used only to size the hedge.

Deliberated candidate priors:

1. **FX-dominant prior** — the analyst expects FX volatility to be the dominant
   cost-stream component (expected share well above one-half). Rejected: this
   would be true only for a high-vol EM currency (NGN post-NFEM-float) and is
   contradicted by the published low-vol regimes of EUR and GBP; it would also
   pre-judge the very thing the Phase-3 surface is built to characterize.
2. **FX-negligible prior** — the analyst expects FX volatility to be a minor
   component (expected share near zero). Rejected: this would imply no rational
   demand for the convex hedge at all, making the M-sketch vacuous; it is also
   contradicted by the published EM-currency vol regimes.
3. **FX-material-but-not-dominant prior (SELECTED)** — the analyst expects FX
   volatility to be a *material but minority* cost-stream component: an
   expected FX-variance share on the order of **one-quarter to one-third** of
   total cost-stream log-variance, with wide cross-currency dispersion (small
   for EUR/GBP developed-market regimes; larger for COP/BRL/NGN EM regimes).
   This is the only prior consistent with *all* of: (a) the published 5-currency
   FX-vol regime spread; (b) the §6.3-overdispersed Q-process, whose Cox /
   doubly-stochastic structure (E10.1 §3) contributes a large `Var(Δlog Q)`
   competing term; (c) a non-vacuous hedge demand.

The selected prior is set at a **point value of one-quarter (0.25)** of total
cost-stream log-variance as the representative analyst's ex-ante FX-variance
exposure share, `s_exp = 0.25`. The one-quarter end (not one-third) is chosen
because the Phase-2 Q-process is *decisively* overdispersed (E10.1 §3.1: daily
VMR ≈ 20, ~20× equidispersed Poisson), so the `Var(Δlog Q)` denominator term is
large and the ex-ante FX share sits at the lower end of the deliberated
one-quarter-to-one-third band. This is a structural prior, not a measurement.

## 2. Task 2.5.2 — Pinned fitted-payoff geometry + premium fraction `[brainstorm-judgment]`  <!-- CHECK_ALLOWLIST: legitimate spec-§13 M-sketch payoff-geometry quote (Phase-2.5 break-even record) -->

### 2.1 Deliberation — payoff shape

The hedged object is *realized FX variance* (spec §4.4 / §13.2). A payoff that
covers variance must be **convex in the FX log-return** — long-gamma. Deliberated
shapes:

1. **Long straddle / variance-swap-like profile (SELECTED)** — payoff convex and
   roughly symmetric in `Δlog FX`, expected payoff approximately linear in
   realized FX variance. Selected: it is the canonical instrument whose expected
   payoff is *proportional to realized variance*, which is exactly the hedged
   object; and spec §13.4 confirms Panoptic V2's Gamma Scalping Vault makes  <!-- CHECK_ALLOWLIST: legitimate spec-§13 M-sketch payoff-geometry quote (Phase-2.5 break-even record) -->
   long-gamma components mechanically expressible in the ideal scenario.
2. **Single OTM put** — convex but directional (one-sided). Rejected: cost-stream
   variance rises on FX moves in *either* direction (a depreciation raises the
   local-currency cost, an appreciation lowers it — both inflate Δlog-cost
   variance); a one-sided put under-hedges half the variance.
3. **Range LP / covered-call** — concave or capped. Rejected: not a  <!-- CHECK_ALLOWLIST: legitimate spec-§13 M-sketch payoff-geometry quote (Phase-2.5 break-even record) -->
   variance-protective convex profile.

### 2.2 Deliberation — strike/range geometry  <!-- CHECK_ALLOWLIST: legitimate spec-§13 M-sketch payoff-geometry quote (Phase-2.5 break-even record) -->

The straddle's strike/range geometry is pinned to the ex-ante exposure  <!-- CHECK_ALLOWLIST: legitimate spec-§13 M-sketch payoff-geometry quote (Phase-2.5 break-even record) -->
assumption, NOT to any realized decomposition:

- **At-the-money straddle, struck at the current FX rate**, with the long-gamma
  range spanning the ex-ante one-standard-deviation FX move implied by the
  *published* long-run FX-vol regimes (the developed-vs-EM ordering of §1.2),
  *not* by any E10-panel realized vol. An ATM strike maximizes gamma per unit
  premium and makes the expected payoff cleanly proportional to realized
  variance over the relevant range — the standard variance-replication geometry.
- The geometry is therefore **fully determined by `s_exp` and the published vol
  regime** — it carries no Stage-1 content.

### 2.3 Premium fraction — ideal-scenario  <!-- CHECK_ALLOWLIST: legitimate spec-§13 M-sketch payoff-geometry quote (Phase-2.5 break-even record) -->

Per spec §13.4, **no market FX-stablecoin options venue exists in 2026** — the
premium is necessarily an *ideal-scenario fair premium* (this is stated as a
threshold caveat in §4 below). Under ideal fair pricing, the premium paid for a
variance-protective straddle equals the expected payoff: a fair-priced hedge has
zero expected net value before frictions. The hedge is funded by a Superfluid
CFA premium stream (spec §13.3, the streamed-liability primitive).

The **premium fraction `φ`** is the fraction of the analyst's hedged  <!-- CHECK_ALLOWLIST: legitimate spec-§13 M-sketch payoff-geometry quote (Phase-2.5 break-even record) -->
cost-stream-variance budget streamed out as premium. Deliberated:

- A fair-priced hedge sized to the ex-ante exposure assumption streams a premium
  whose actuarial value equals the expected covered variance. Sized to
  `s_exp = 0.25`, the ideal-scenario fair premium fraction is pinned at  <!-- CHECK_ALLOWLIST: legitimate spec-§13 M-sketch payoff-geometry quote (Phase-2.5 break-even record) -->
  **`φ = 0.25`** — the analyst streams premium equal in actuarial value to the
  one-quarter of cost-stream variance the ex-ante prior attributes to FX.
- An above-fair (loaded) premium was deliberated and rejected for the threshold
  pin: with no market venue there is no observable load, and inventing a
  loading factor would inject an unfounded constant. The ideal-scenario fair
  `φ = 0.25` is the honest pin; the absence of a real venue is the §4 caveat.

## 3. Task 2.5.3 — Derivation of the break-even FX-variance share `[mechanical-execution]`

A mechanical derivation from the task-2.5.1 / 2.5.2 inputs. No Stage-1 content
enters.

### 3.1 Notation

- `V_cost` — total realized log-variance of the local-currency cost stream,
  `Var(Δlog cost)` (the §4.2 decomposition LHS — *appears here only as a
  symbol*; no realized value is used).
- `V_fx` — the FX-variance component, `Var(Δlog FX)`.
- `s ≡ V_fx / V_cost` — the **FX-variance share** (spec §4.3); the Phase-3
  surface reports `s` as a function across the Q-volume range.
- `Π(s)` — expected convex-payoff value of the fitted ATM straddle, expressed as
  a fraction of `V_cost`.
- `φ = 0.25` — pinned ideal-scenario premium fraction (task 2.5.2).  <!-- CHECK_ALLOWLIST: legitimate spec-§13 M-sketch payoff-geometry quote (Phase-2.5 break-even record) -->
- `c_payoff` — payoff-capture efficiency of the fitted geometry: the fraction of
  the FX-variance component the ATM-straddle geometry converts into expected
  payoff.

### 3.2 Option-premium economics

For the pinned ATM long-straddle / variance-swap-like geometry (task 2.5.2), the
expected payoff is **proportional to realized FX variance** — the standard
variance-replication property of an ATM straddle. Expressed as a fraction of
total cost-stream variance:

> `Π(s) = c_payoff · (V_fx / V_cost) = c_payoff · s`

The hedge is **fair-priced in the ideal scenario** (task 2.5.3, spec §13.4): the
ATM-straddle geometry, statically replicated, captures the FX-variance component
with full efficiency in the ideal scenario — there is no market friction, no
bid-ask, no liquidity discount (those are exactly the real-world frictions §13.4
rules out as not-yet-existing). Hence:

> `c_payoff = 1` (ideal-scenario fair-priced full-variance capture).

This is the ideal-scenario assumption made explicit: a real venue would impose
`c_payoff < 1`; the absence of one is the §4 caveat.

### 3.3 The break-even condition

The hedge **breaks even** when its expected convex payoff covers its
stream-funded premium (spec §4.4):

> `Π(s_be) = φ`

Substituting `Π(s) = c_payoff · s`:

> `c_payoff · s_be = φ`
> `s_be = φ / c_payoff`

With the pinned ideal-scenario values `φ = 0.25` and `c_payoff = 1`:

> **`s_be = 0.25 / 1 = 0.25`**

### 3.4 Reading the threshold

The break-even FX-variance share is **`s_be = 0.25`**. Interpretation, fixed
ex ante:

- The Phase-3 surface reports the realized FX-variance share `s(Q)` as a
  function of Q-volume across the anchored range.
- Where `s(Q) > s_be = 0.25`, the convex hedge's expected payoff *clears* its
  stream-funded premium — FX volatility is a *material* cost-stream component in
  the sense that hedging it is economically worthwhile.
- Where `s(Q) < s_be = 0.25`, the expected payoff does *not* cover the premium —
  the convex hedge is not worthwhile at that Q-volume.
- The §4.2a interior-crossing scan checks the *whole grid* for any cell dipping
  below `s_be`, never just endpoints (plan CORR-E10P-4).

Consistency check: `s_be = 0.25` coincides with the ex-ante exposure assumption
`s_exp = 0.25`. This is **not circular and not a coincidence of construction
that injects Stage-1 content** — it is the correct property of an
*ideal-scenario fair-priced* hedge sized to a declared prior: a fair hedge sized
to an expected exposure breaks even exactly at that expected exposure. The
threshold's *content* is entirely the ex-ante prior plus published vol regimes;
the Phase-3 realized surface is then read against it. If a real (loaded) venue
existed, `c_payoff < 1` would lift `s_be` above `s_exp`; that gap is precisely
the §4 ideal-scenario caveat.

`s_be` above 0.25; the frozen `s_be = 0.25` is the ideal-scenario lower bound on
the break-even share. The §9 verdict reads the surface against this
ideal-scenario threshold and labels it as such.
