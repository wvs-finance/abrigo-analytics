## 3. Overdispersion mechanism (task 2.3a)

### 3.1 The finding — Q is decisively overdispersed

Variance-to-mean ratio (VMR; equidispersed Poisson ⇒ VMR = 1):

| Aggregation | Centre VMR | Bracket VMR |
|---|---|---|
| Per calendar day (zero-filled) | **20.6** | **19.8** |
| Per ISO week | 15.0 | 29.1 |
| Per session | 4.2 | 6.0 |
| Per 5-minute window | 4.6 | 5.3 |

Daily-count VMR ≈ 20 — **~20× the equidispersed-Poisson value of 1.0** — and the
centre and bracket reach this *independently*. A homogeneous or even a smoothly
deterministically-modulated NHPP has a Poisson count law (equidispersed
conditional on λ(t)) and cannot reproduce this. An explicit overdispersion
mechanism is **required, not optional** — this directly settles plan task 2.3a /
CORR-E10P-2 / Model QA S-1.

### 3.2 Candidate deliberation `[brainstorm-judgment]`

Three standard, defensible mechanisms:

1. **Cox / doubly-stochastic NHPP** — λ(t) itself is a random process (e.g. a
   Gamma- or log-normal-distributed sprint-intensity multiplier on the
   deterministic envelope). Overdispersion arises from rate variability; the
   same mechanism *endogenously* produces the §6.3-item-2 burstiness and the
   §6.3-item-3 event spikes.
2. **Mixed-Poisson / negative-binomial aggregate marginal** — a single Gamma
   dispersion parameter on the monthly count. Cleanest one-parameter fit to a
   ~20× VMR; matches the marginal exactly.
3. **Self-exciting (Hawkes) clustering kernel** — each arrival raises the
   intensity of near-future arrivals. Produces clustering through endogenous
   contagion.

### 3.3 Decision — LOCK: Cox / doubly-stochastic NHPP

> **Reference:** Spec v0.6 §6.3 item 2 (overdispersion / burstiness
> pre-commitment) and §6.3 closing sentence (functional forms locked in the
> E10.1 calibration note); plan task 2.3a / CORR-E10P-2 / Model QA S-1;
> extraction §6 (VMR table + structured-burst evidence — 5-min windows of
> 16-24 calls, discrete 27/42-query spike days). Cox, D.R. (1955),
> doubly-stochastic / mixed Poisson processes — the canonical overdispersed
> point-process family.
>
> **Why this mechanism:** the trace's overdispersion is **structured, not
> featureless**. It is not a flat inflation of the variance — it is organized
> as 5-minute windows of 16-24 calls and discrete 27/42-query spike days
> against 1-6-query background. A bare negative-binomial count law (candidate 2)
> reproduces the ~20× *marginal* VMR but is silent on *within-day clustering* —
> it would require a separate, disconnected mechanism for §6.3 items 2 and 3.
> A Hawkes kernel (candidate 3) produces clustering but its self-excitation is
> an endogenous-contagion story unsupported by the trace: the bursts are driven
> by exogenous research/build sprints (the 05-08 and 05-18 spike days align
> with discrete E10-revision and prior-iteration spec sprints), not by queries
> triggering queries. A Cox process with a Gamma-mixed sprint-intensity λ(t)
> reproduces the ~20× daily VMR, the within-day burst structure, AND the event
> spikes from **one** mechanism, and matches the exogenous-sprint micro-story
> the trace shows.
>
> **Relevance to E10 v0.6:** the overdispersion mechanism directly inflates
> `Var(Δlog Q)` — the denominator term that competes with `Var(Δlog FX)` in the
> §4.3 FX-variance-share surface. A mechanism that under-states Q's variance
> would spuriously inflate the FX share; one that over-states it would
> spuriously deflate it. The Cox route ties Q's variance to an *observed*
> sprint-intensity structure rather than a free dispersion knob, keeping the
> §4.3 surface honest. The §6.3 modulations 2 and 3 are then not independent
> free parameters but two readouts of one calibrated process.
>
> **Connection to the simulator:** the chosen mechanism is the overdispersion
> slot the R6 NHPP engine (plan task 2.1) is built against — a stochastic
> sprint-intensity layer multiplying the deterministic λ(t) envelope of §4.
> Plan task 0.5's failing-test harness exercises this layer; the §4.3
> surface-grid evaluator (task 0.7) consumes the Q-variance it generates.

### 3.4 Note for the pre-Phase-2 review

The extraction independently recommended the Cox / doubly-stochastic route over
a bare negative-binomial, on the same structured-burst grounds. The choice is
**load-bearing** (it sets the §4.3 denominator) but the trace evidence points
decisively and one-directionally to the Cox route — the candidates are not
genuinely balanced. **It is locked here, not flagged for separate user
confirmation**, and is surfaced to the mandatory pre-Phase-2 RC + Model QA
review for ratification of the decision-citation (per §7 review hook). If the
review reads the choice as still open, the fallback is candidate 2 (NB marginal)
with a separately-specified burst layer — recorded here so the fallback is not
a fresh design step.

## 4. The five λ(t) modulations (task 2.3)

The R6 NHPP intensity λ(t) is pre-committed (§6.3) to carry five modulations.
This section locks the functional form of each. Modulations 1, 3, 5 and the
burst layer of 2 are own-trace-anchored; modulation 4 is proxy-bracket-pending.

### 4.1 Modulation 1 — diurnal + weekday/weekend cycle — LOCKED

**Form:** multiplicative working-hours envelope = (weekday indicator with a
~95%/~5% weekday/weekend weight) × (a **two-peak** diurnal shape — sum of two
Gaussian bumps centred on the UTC-morning and UTC-evening working blocks), NOT a
single Gaussian.

> **Reference:** Spec §6.3 item 1; extraction §5.1 (centre 62/63 weekday =
> 98.4%; bracket 142/151 = 94%; combined ≈ 95%; centre diurnal clusters UTC
> 11-12h with 33 calls and 21-22h with 23 calls — `e10_q_anchor_buckets.json`
> `diurnal_utc` / `weekday`).
> **Why:** the trace shows an unambiguous weekday concentration and a clearly
> *bimodal* daily shape (two working blocks), so a single-bump diurnal form
> would mis-place arrival mass.
> **Relevance to E10 v0.6:** the diurnal/weekday cycle is intra-month structure;
> monthly-aggregate Q averages over it, so it shifts the §4.3 share only weakly,
> but mis-specifying it as unimodal would distort the sub-monthly differencing
> grid the exact §4.2 decomposition is computed on (CORR-E10P-3).
> **Connection to the simulator:** this is the day-type × hour-block seasonality
> grid of the R6 engine (plan task 2.1); plan task 0.5 fixtures exercise it.

### 4.2 Modulation 2 — burstiness / overdispersion — LOCKED (see §3)

**Form:** the Gamma-mixed sprint-intensity layer of the Cox process locked in §3
— burstiness and overdispersion are two readouts of one mechanism, not a
separate functional form. Evidence: 5-min windows up to 16 (centre) / 24
(bracket) calls; within-session inter-arrival median 1.9 s (centre). The §3
decision-citation covers this modulation; no separate citation is repeated here.

### 4.3 Modulation 3 — event-driven spikes — LOCKED

**Form:** a multiplicative spike term that fires on **discrete days** (a sparse
Bernoulli day-marker with a heavy-tailed multiplier), NOT a continuous
modulation. In the Cox framing this is the upper tail of the sprint-intensity
process — the same mechanism as §3, realized as whole-day spikes.

> **Reference:** Spec §6.3 item 3; extraction §5.3 (centre spike days 27/27 on
> 2026-05-08 and 2026-05-18 against background 8 and 1; bracket spike days 42
> and 26 against background 2-6; spikes align with discrete research/build
> pushes).
> **Why:** the per-day distribution is spike-dominated, not smooth — a
> continuous modulation cannot reproduce two 27-query days against a 1-query
> day. The spike is discrete and event-triggered.
> **Relevance to E10 v0.6:** event spikes are a primary contributor to
> `Var(Δlog Q)` and hence to the §4.3 denominator; modelling them as continuous
> would smear the variance and bias the FX share upward.
> **Connection to the simulator:** the spike day-marker is a slot in the R6
> engine's λ(t); plan task 0.5 fixtures exercise it; the variance it injects is
> consumed by the §4.3 surface-grid evaluator.

### 4.4 Modulation 4 — month-end reporting seasonality — OPEN (proxy-bracket-pending)

**Form (committed):** a multiplicative end-of-month bump on λ(t) — pre-committed
by §6.3 item 4 and retained. **Magnitude: NOT resolvable from the own-trace.**

> **Reference:** Spec §6.3 item 4 and §6.2 (the bracket of the range is set by
> researched proxies); extraction §5.4 / §8 item 1.
> **Why open:** the centre trace spans only ~11 days inside a single month
> (2026-05) — a month-end pattern cannot be resolved from the centre. The
> bracket spans one month boundary (2026-04 → 2026-05) and shows activity near
> the 2026-04 month-end (04-26/04-27: 24 queries), but ~27 days of span across
> one boundary is too short to *isolate* a reporting-cadence bump from ordinary
> sprint clustering.
> **Relevance to E10 v0.6:** month-end seasonality is monthly-aggregate
> structure; an over-large magnitude would add spurious between-month
> `Var(Δlog Q)` and deflate the §4.3 FX share. It is therefore left as an
> explicit OPEN calibration item rather than force-pinned.
> **Connection to the simulator:** the modulation-4 slot exists in the R6 λ(t);
> its magnitude is set from the §6.2 proxy bracket (The Graph public
> query-volume statistics / analyst-tooling telemetry) at plan task 2.2's
> proxy-research step, NOT from this trace. Per CORR-E10P-8 / RC WEAK-2: if the
> proxies are unreachable the bracket cannot form → PARTIAL per §9; no
> flat-fill, no arbitrary magnitude.

This is the one of the five modulations the own-trace cannot pin. It is a
**non-HALT, spec-anticipated** OPEN item — §6.2 explicitly routes bracket
magnitudes to proxy research.

### 4.5 Modulation 5 — slow secular workload drift — LOCKED

**Form:** a slow monotone trend term on baseline λ(t) over the panel window — a
low-order (log-linear) drift, calibrated off the bracket's monotone weekly rise.

> **Reference:** Spec §6.3 item 5; extraction §5.5 (bracket per-week counts rise
> monotonically W16:2 → W17:6 → W18:25 → W19:44 → W20:74 over 5 weeks).
> **Why:** the bracket shows a clear, monotone upward drift in weekly

> short and too sprint-dominated to read a slope, so the drift *slope* is
> bracket-anchored while the centre confirms non-stationarity.
> **Relevance to E10 v0.6:** secular drift is the lowest-frequency component of
> `Var(Δlog Q)`; over a ~30-month panel it contributes between-month variance
> and so enters the §4.3 denominator. A log-linear form keeps it parsimonious
> and avoids over-fitting a slope to a 5-week window.
> **Connection to the simulator:** the drift term is the baseline-λ(t) trend
> slot of the R6 engine; its slope is calibrated from the bracket weekly series.

## 5. Q_low (task 2.3b / spec §2.4)

`Q_low` is the **qualitative-limit threshold** — the query volume at which the
free tier becomes suboptimal because of its *non-numeric* deficiencies (rate
caps; no `archive`/`trace_*` access; single-subgraph coverage; no cross-protocol
curation), NOT because the 100k/mo free quota is exhausted (§2.4).

**§2.4 pinning rule (re-stated):** `Q_low` is set as the volume at which the
user's own observed query logs first exhibit a qualitative pattern the free tier
cannot serve, corroborated by bracketing proxy research; the value and its
derivation are pinned at E10.1 and recorded in this note.

**Status: OPEN — proxy-bracket-pending.** The own-trace gives the Q-volume
*centre* (§2) but does **not** pin `Q_low`:

- The mapping (§1.1) records only a coarse target (URL host / `mcp__server__op`)
  per call — by the metadata-only guarantee it never stores query arguments or
  response payloads. The trace therefore cannot show whether a given query
  *needed* an `archive`/`trace_*` capability or cross-subgraph curation — the
  qualitative free-tier deficiencies §2.4 keys on are not observable in the
  extracted metadata.
- `Q_low` is a *behavioral* parameter (the volume at which qualitative
  deficiencies bind); pinning it requires the §2.4-mandated corroborating proxy
  research (free-tier qualitative-limit characterizations), not the own-trace
  alone — exactly as extraction §8 item 2 records.

`Q_low` is therefore recorded as an **OPEN, non-HALT, spec-anticipated** item:
its value is pinned at plan task 2.3b from the §2.4 rule against the proxy
research at task 2.2. Per CORR-E10P-8 / RC WEAK-2, if the proxies are
unreachable the bracket cannot form → PARTIAL per §9; no flat-fill.
